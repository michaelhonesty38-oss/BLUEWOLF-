Order a ride

Set your pickup and drop-off on the map and see the fare first. Watch your driver arrive live, and get an alert when the driver is waiting at the pickup.

Order a delivery

Send a parcel across town. Take a photo of the item, choose the size, and follow it on the map. The driver takes a photo when it is delivered.

Shops marketplace

Browse supermarkets and shops that Blue Wolf has signed up in person. Pick the goods, pay online, and a delivery agent brings them to your door. Shop not listed? Ask a driver to buy for you.

Customers

Drivers

  • Sign up as a driver in the same app. Every driver is checked by hand before approval
  • Go online and accept nearby requests
  • Live navigation view to the pickup, the store and the drop-off
  • Paid into your wallet after the customer and you both confirm
  • See your own trips and monthly progress. Switch to customer any time, one side at a time

Built to protect both sides, even on poor network

Photo proof

Deliveries start with a photo of the item and end with a photo of the delivered item.

Both confirm

The driver is paid and the commission taken only after the driver and the customer each tap confirm.

Eyes on every trip

Customer and driver positions are shared during a trip and kept as a trail Blue Wolf can check.

Works offline

With no signal the trip stays active and the money stays held. Taps and GPS points are saved on the phone and sent when the network returns, and a location can go to Blue Wolf by SMS.

The pilot

Start at Issele-Asaba Junction, then expand across Asaba

Proposed corridor · demand and fares need field validation

Proposed pilot area

Issele-Asaba Junction on the Asaba–Benin Expressway

Blue Wolf concentrates its first drivers and daily commuters around this corridor instead of launching across the whole city.

Junction→Adjoining Asaba routes→Wider city coverage→More cities and delivery later

Launch sequence

  1. Map the main commuter destinations from the junction.
  2. Measure passenger volumes, current fares and peak waiting times.
  3. Recruit and position drivers around the operating area.
  4. Run a controlled pilot in peak commuting periods.
  5. Measure pickup time, completion and repeat use.
  6. Expand to adjoining routes once pilot targets are met.

Supply track

  • Recruit local drivers
  • Verify driver and vehicle
  • Explain commission and protection
  • Position supply around peak periods
  • Offer a clear, fair earnings split and tracked payouts

Demand track

  • Target one commuter corridor
  • Reach workplaces and residential clusters
  • Run local referral activity
  • Convert first riders into repeat users

Safety and shops

Tracked only when it matters

Location tracking

  • Location comes from three sources: the vehicle tracker, the driver’s phone and the passenger’s phone
  • Drivers are tracked only while they are Online (Active) and during a trip
  • Passengers are tracked only during a trip, and at no other time
  • Location is used for emergencies, tracking and preventing illegal activity, and is seen by the admin only
  • Driver tracking, tracker registration and the tamper alert are built, and Blue Wolf switches them on at launch. The tracker hardware is still to be bought
  • A driver who removes the tracker, or stops it showing on the admin screen while Active, is suspended from the app and reported to law enforcement agencies

Blue Wolf Shop

  • Shops list their products with photos and a full item list, so customers see what they are buying
  • Shop owners add their own photos, prices and item lists in the app (built, switched on by Blue Wolf at launch). Blue Wolf can also add them from the admin page
  • Customers pay by bank transfer, held by Blue Wolf until delivery is confirmed
  • Blue Wolf earns a commission on the sale, not the whole sale value

Business model

  • The 20% platform commission (driver keeps 80%) is a starting figure. Blue Wolf keeps at least 15% of each fare and will add one or two more insurance partners alongside Myka, for comprehensive and mechanical cover
  • Blue Wolf sets the final percentage when it agrees terms with its insurers. No partnership has been made yet; discussions are still being arranged
  • Payment and banking partners cannot be assessed until funding is received and the pilot gives real numbers
  • The same app also supports deliveries and partner-shop orders, which are built but unproven
  • No profit, return or exit is promised or implied
  • Blue Wolf is a transport marketplace. It does not trade or hold cryptocurrency

Main risks

  • Regulation: permits for ride-hailing in Delta State may be required and not yet confirmed
  • Safety incidents, and public trust in a new brand
  • Drivers or passengers may not use the app, or may leave it for cash and phone calls
  • Fuel, vehicle and cost changes, and the naira exchange rate
  • Insurance and payment partners may take longer than planned or not agree
  • Small team and a single founder
  • Any new city needs its own permits, partners and legal and payment-compliance review
